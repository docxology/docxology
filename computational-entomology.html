Computational Entomology: Algorithms, Models, and Digital Insect Colonies

Author: Daniel Ari Friedman, PhD Affiliation: Active Inference Institute Last updated: 2026-10-21 Domain: Entomology

Computational entomology uses quantitative analysis, simulation, and bioinformatics to study insects. Models can connect individual behavior with colony dynamics, but their biological interpretation depends on the observations, assumptions, and validation available for each study.

What Evidence Connects Physiology With Collective Foraging?

The physiology of forager hydration and variation among harvester ant (Pogonomyrmex barbatus) colonies in collective foraging behavior (Friedman, Greene & Gordon, 2019) reports that hydrated harvester ant foragers made more trips than unhydrated nestmates, with hydration and desiccation responses varying among colonies. Gene expression variation in the brains of harvester ant foragers is associated with collective behavior (Friedman et al., 2020) reports associations between brain gene expression and collective behavior. An association between expression and colony behavior does not alone establish a causal molecular mechanism.

How Can Models Explain Colony Behavior?

A single-pheromone model accounts for empirical patterns of ant colony foraging previously modeled using two pheromones (Saund & Friedman, 2023) shows that a single-pheromone model can account for empirical patterns previously addressed with more complex pheromone models. Computational Complexity and Energetics of the Ant Stack (Friedman, 2025) studies a particular Ant Stack framework. Its computational and energy analysis concerns that biomimetic architecture and its assumptions.

Which Software Projects Are Relevant?

BeeStack: An Evidence-Typed Scaffold for Whole-Colony Honeybee Simulation (Friedman & Chambers, 2026) presents a scaffold for honeybee simulation with explicit evidence distinctions. Ento-Linguistics: Language, Ambiguity, and Scientific Communication in Entomology (Friedman & Chambers, 2026) concerns language and scientific communication in entomology. COGANT: Deterministic Codebase-to-GNN Translation (Friedman, 2026) describes codebase-to-GNN translation; it is a computational tool rather than an empirical insect model.

Project repositories include BeeStack, Ento-Linguistics, MetaInformAnt, and Active_InferAnts. Their implementation and validation scopes differ; consult the project documentation before choosing one.

What Does Neurogenomics Add?

The Role of Dopamine in the Collective Regulation of Foraging in Harvester Ants (Friedman et al., 2018) reports experiments on dopamine and foraging in harvester ants. Behavioral, physiological, and transcriptomic variation among colonies of the red harvester ant (Pogonomyrmex barbatus) (Friedman, 2019) brings together behavioral, physiological, and transcriptomic variation among colonies. Combining these measurements can motivate models, but a calibrated prediction from molecular measurements to whole-colony behavior requires its own validation.

How Should Biological and Engineering Claims Be Compared?

A simulation that reproduces a behavioral pattern is evidence about that model under its tested conditions. It does not establish that insects implement the same algorithm or that a related robotic system will behave reliably. Compare the model assumptions, measured outputs, negative controls, and available code before transferring a result to another setting.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is computational entomology?

Computational entomology applies quantitative analysis, computational models, and bioinformatics to insect biology and behavior.

Are the listed projects complete biological simulations?

They have different purposes and evidence boundaries. A software scaffold or synthetic model should not be treated as a validated whole-organism or whole-colony simulation.

Where can I find publications?

The Entomology domain index includes journal articles and other research outputs. Zenodo is a repository; deposition there does not establish peer review.
