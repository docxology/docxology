Findings and contributions

  • The authors state the document's purpose is only to justify the proposed flow, which is a posit testable only once its components exist.
  • They list missing components, including sim, CYC and chem tables, the CYC modulator (a process reward model), a trigger table and a sim resolver.
  • The authors argue a process reward model is critical: without it, it would be impossible to check which route or CYC completion was better.
  • The authors flag as untested whether the proposed decreasing-slot protocol for breaking down CYCs is valid or runs smoothly.

Methods

  • Independent vision-processing modules (Scanning Trio, Track Trio) — Proposes organ-like modules (modScan/modLoc/modNav for maps, points, routes; modWatch/modLook/modTude for shading, attention, tests) that process vision input and publish triggers.
  • Chempool emulation of accumulated chemicals/hormones — Uses chempool nodes attached to modules to tally stacked chemical notations (e.g. STRESS^, #ERROR) that time and combine trigger initiation.
  • CYC drivers decomposed into |n| slots by a CYC modulator — Epigenetic-emulation CYC drivers (e.g. WAKE-HAPPY-WAKE) are split into six-slot blocks force-filled from priority tables set by the CYC modulator, then resolved.
  • modStream trigger grading into themes, latches and simulations — Triggers from the vision trios (and CYCs) are graded by modStream into themes and latches (simulations), which modLatch resolves into updates.
  • Planned DEMO 0.01 with hardcoded CYCs and simulations — Proposes a demo that handwrites 11 CYCs and a simple map simulation, 'cheats' the PRM step, and shows chem accumulation and trigger selection without learning.
