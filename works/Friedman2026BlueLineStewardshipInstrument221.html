The Blue Line: A Stewardship Instrument for Maintained Commitments

Daniel Ari Friedman

Zenodo

Download PDF Publication source Paper folder on GitHub Read extracted text

Overview

A stewardship instrument for maintained commitments: systems, obligations, and relationships to past work. It reads a declared file of commitments against a versioned registry and returns a fail-closed declaration-coverage verdict (MAINTAINED, NEEDS_ATTENTION, STALE, OUTSIDE_SCOPE) over the care signals each commitment requires. It measures whether the care a steward declared was fresh at the review date — never whether the care happened; a MAINTAINED verdict is not a warranty, SLA, availability guarantee, or proof of maintenance, and it never authorizes an action. Findings and contributions

  • An empty scan set (no matching commitment or unreadable file) returns OUTSIDE_SCOPE with notes and never yields MAINTAINED.
  • The stated staleness-monotonicity property holds that the stale set is always a subset of the missing set.
  • The verdict is a declaration-coverage statement, explicitly not a warranty, SLA, availability guarantee or proof of maintenance.
  • The author notes the instrument only reads declarations and does not inspect repositories, run checks or contact anyone; the freshness window is a policy constant, not empirical.

Methods

  • Versioned registry of twelve commitments with SHA-256 digest pinning — Commitments are frozen records (id, title, wire, tags, required care signals, kind) whose sorted canonical form is hashed and pinned in every reading.
  • Staged, fail-closed intake normalization of declared files — Declared files are normalized; malformed tags or undated signals are set aside as intake notes rather than causing crashes or invented values.
  • Freshness-window evaluation into MAINTAINED / NEEDS_ATTENTION / STALE — Required signals of tag-matched commitments are compared with signals declared within a freshness window (180 days by default) to assign a verdict.
  • Formal definition of readings, commitments and fresh sets — A reading is formalised as a tuple and properties (fail-closed, staleness monotonicity, digest pinning) are stated.
  • Worked examples: maintained quarter, stale repository, unmatched scan — Three illustrative declared files show the verdicts the instrument returns; these are examples, not an empirical evaluation.
