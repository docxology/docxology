Research Synthesis & Foundational Guide

What Is Cognitive Security? Theory, Threat Models, and Multi-Agent Defense

Author: Daniel Ari Friedman, PhD Affiliation: Active Inference Institute Last updated: 2026-10-21 Domain: Cognitive Security

Cognitive security concerns the integrity of sensemaking and decision-making by people, organizations, and artificial agents. This guide connects research on information environments with proposals for documenting and checking claims. A conceptual framework, a computational demonstration, and a deployed defense provide different kinds of evidence.

What Is Cognitive Security?

Structuring the Information Commons: Open Standards and Cognitive Security (David, Cordes & Friedman, eds., 2022) examines open standards and cognitive security in information commons. Bridging gaps in image meme research: A multidisciplinary paradigm for scaling up qualitative analyses (Mascarenhas, Friedman & Cordes, 2024) proposes a multidisciplinary research approach to image memes. These works offer ways to study information environments; they do not establish that every meme is malicious or that a single intervention secures public discourse.

How Does Cognitive Security Relate to Information Security?

Information security addresses properties such as confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data and systems. Cognitive security also asks how information is interpreted and used. Accurate transmission alone does not establish that a claim is true, that its context is complete, or that a decision is well supported.

Which Publications Address Narratives and Collaborative Analysis?

Reimagining Maps (Cordes, Friedman & Maron, 2020) considers mapping practices. Narrative Information Management (Cordes et al., 2021) addresses narrative information management. Emergent Teams for Complex Threats (Cordes & Friedman, 2020) and The Facilitator's Catechism Playbook (Friedman & Cordes, 2021) discuss collaborative work and facilitation. Together these publications address mapping, information management, teamwork, and facilitation.

How Can Active Inference Inform Cognitive Security?

Active Inferants: An Active Inference Framework for Ant Colony Behavior (Friedman et al., 2021) applies Active Inference to ant colony behavior. It illustrates how generative models can be used to describe adaptive behavior; its ant models do not validate a human influence-defense system. Applying related concepts to cognitive security requires explicit models, threat assumptions, and evaluation in the intended setting.

What Do Formal and Computational Frameworks Establish?

Cognitive Integrity Framework: Formal Foundations (Part 1 of 3: Theoretical Foundations) (Friedman, 2026) develops a formal framework. White Line: A Typed Ledger for the Edge of the Claim (Friedman, 2026) and Personal Red Lines for Development (Friedman, 2026) address claim boundaries and development constraints. Cognitive Diagrams: Reviewing Categorical Accounts of Linguistic Case (Friedman, 2026) reviews categorical accounts of linguistic case with bounded synthetic illustrations. These sources do not demonstrate immunity to prompt injection or guarantee the correctness of deployed agents.

How Can Claims Be Made Easier to Check?

ATLAS: A Question Oriented Approach to the Use of Pattern Languages in Knowledge Management (Cordes et al., 2023) addresses question-oriented pattern languages in knowledge management. On this site, the citation guide and evidence ledger expose sources and verification boundaries. A source link enables inspection; it does not by itself prove that the adjacent claim follows from the source.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is cognitive security?

Cognitive security concerns the integrity of sensemaking and decision-making, including how people and agents assess information and respond to manipulation.

Does a formal framework guarantee secure agents?

A formal result applies to its stated model and assumptions. Deployment security requires separate evaluation against concrete threats.
