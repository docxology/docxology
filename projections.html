Projections

One surface over everything I have externalized — the positioning statement behind this site, and the case for treating a public record of thinking as an asset in its own right.

One surface, many projections. This is the temple I've built.

This site is one navigable surface laid over everything I have externalized: the essays and papers, the talks and recordings, the software, the drawings, the conversations. Each page here is a projection of that underlying body of work — a view from a particular angle, at a particular time. The bibliography projects the publications. The art catalog projects the drawings. The evidence ledger projects the claims. None of them is the thing itself; the thing itself is the whole record, and this surface is how you walk through it.

I built it this way deliberately. Rather than scattering myself across platforms and profiles, I have gathered the work onto one surface I control — a temple in the old sense: a place where what matters is kept, ordered, and open to visitors.

The record, not the snapshot

For roughly two decades I have been externalizing my thinking with unusual consistency — essays, papers, tweets, forum posts, conversations, recorded lectures and interviews. Any single artifact from that stream is a snapshot. But the stream itself is something different: a longitudinal record of how a mind interacts with reality. What you believed. Why you believed it. Where you were wrong. What changed your mind. Which ideas kept recurring, decade after decade, and how the worldview evolved around them.

A résumé compresses. A book distills. But a longitudinal record preserves the process: the updates and the reversals are part of the asset, not noise to be edited out. You can watch a person think.

It compounds

Something shifts when the record gets long and the models get capable. The corpus becomes an external representation of cognition — not just an archive of outputs, but a working model of the cognition that produced them. A book captures a snapshot; an external brain captures the trajectory.

Over decades you accumulate judgment, frameworks, pattern recognition, mistakes, updates, context. Each year of externalizing makes the whole more useful to the models that read it and to the people who build on it. The asset is no longer any single work — it is the interfaces to the underlying thing: the surface, the indexes, the agent routes, the data layer. This site is those interfaces.

Sourcery

Sourcery is the semantic layer on top. It turns this record into scalable applications, using the strengths of LLMs as leverage: retrieval, synthesis, and reasoning applied to a corpus that is already structured, cited, and machine-readable. The words were made public one at a time; Sourcery makes them compositional — the same record, now callable.

Cognitive capital & the intellectual estate

A documented cognitive archive is a form of capital. It need not disappear when production stops. I think people may one day pay to interact with an accumulated way of thinking — to query it, extend it, put it to work — and the estate plan for a mind like mine is to make that possible by default: the record is built to keep teaching after I stop producing. That is what this surface is for, and why it is maintained as an estate rather than a feed.

To walk the record itself, start with Start Here, browse the works, watch the videos, look at the art, and read the publications. Everything above is projections of what you will find there.
