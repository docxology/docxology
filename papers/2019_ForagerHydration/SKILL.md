# The physiology of forager hydration and variation among harvester ant (Pogonomyrmex barbatus) colonies in collective foraging behavior

**Daniel A. Friedman, Michael J. Greene, Deborah M. Gordon** (2019) · Entomology

## Context

This work addresses topics in **Entomology**: Pogonomyrmex barbatus, desiccation physiology, foraging behavior, water balance.

## Methods

Primary methods and techniques applied in this work:

- **Field hydration manipulation of marked P. barbatus foragers** — Foragers from five colonies were cold-anesthetized, paint-marked by treatment, given 0.2 μL water or none, returned to the nest, and their next-day foraging trips counted.
- **Vapor Pressure Deficit regression of hydrated/unhydrated trip ratio** — Field temperature and humidity (iButton) were combined into VPD and regressed (linear vs quadratic, AIC/ANOVA) against the ratio of hydrated to unhydrated trips.
- **Laboratory desiccation assays on 74 foragers from 24 colonies** — Ants were held over Drierite at about 40 °C and weighed every 20 minutes, recording time to morbidity and death, blinded, to derive water loss rates and water contents.
- **Colony grouping by offspring success and foraging regulation** — Colonies were classified by prior parentage-analysis offspring status and by whether they reduce foraging in dry conditions, then compared with Wilcoxon and Chi-square tests.
- **PCA of five water-loss physiology measures** — Principal Component Analysis (factoextra in R) on water contents, times to morbidity and death, and area-independent water loss rate.

## Key Findings

Core contributions and results:

- Pooled across five colonies, hydrated ants made 1.18-fold more foraging trips than unhydrated nestmates (paired Wilcoxon p = 0.0006).
- The hydration effect on foraging grew with desiccation risk (VPD), with a quadratic model fitting better than linear, supporting a non-linear effect.
- Foragers from colonies that reduce foraging in dry conditions lost water faster and reached morbidity and death sooner, without differing in total or critical water content.
- Surprisingly, colonies with offspring colonies had foragers with lower desiccation tolerance, which the authors suggest may reflect more cautious, water-conserving foraging.
- The authors infer that an ant's threshold response to olfactory interactions that trigger foraging is influenced by its current hydration level.
