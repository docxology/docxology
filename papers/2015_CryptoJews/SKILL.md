# Commentary: Portuguese crypto-Jews: the genetic heritage of a complex history

**Alexander W. Marcus, Emily R. Ebel, Daniel A. Friedman** (2015) · Genetics & Biomedical

## Context

This work addresses topics in **Genetics & Biomedical**: Jewish genetics, haplotypes, crypto-Jews, Iberian Peninsula.

## Methods

Primary methods and techniques applied in this work:

- **Critical commentary on Nogueiro et al. (2015) uniparental-marker study** — Evaluates the Y-chromosome and mtDNA evidence and inferences of Nogueiro et al. on Portuguese crypto-Jews via two stated critiques.
- **Comparison against prior work on Jewish haplotype motifs** — Uses Tofanelli et al. (2014) and related studies to assess whether the cited haplotypes are diagnostic of Jewish ancestry.

## Key Findings

Core contributions and results:

- The authors argue the reviewed data are ambiguous for inferring Jewish ancestry and do not identify diagnostic patrilineal or matrilineal markers.
- Many 'Jewish' haplotypes cited are pan-Middle Eastern markers also common in Arabic lineages, so their ancestry is ambiguous.
- The commentary contends the 'complex mating strategies' hypothesis was not tested against demographic or admixture models, and small uniparental samples cannot support robust conclusions.
- The authors propose that admixture in the Bragança Jews and a bottleneck in the Belmonte Jews could be statistically evaluated with existing demographic models.
- They conclude the results offer no concrete support for specific claims about the mating habits, beliefs, or praxis of these historical Jewish populations.
