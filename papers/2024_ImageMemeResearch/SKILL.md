# Bridging gaps in image meme research: A multidisciplinary paradigm

**Mridula Mascarenhas, Daniel Ari Friedman, Richard J Cordes** (2024) · Cognitive Security

## Context

This work addresses topics in **Cognitive Security**: image memes, meme research, digital communication, narrative analysis.

## Methods

Primary methods and techniques applied in this work:

- **Toulmin claim-evidence-warrant analysis of individual image memes** — Applies Toulmin's argument model to illustrative memes about the 2023 East Palestine, Ohio train derailment (e.g., the G.W. Bush/9/11 template and a Bill Murray meme).
- **DRE3 three-tier model: Instrumental, Rhetorical, and Hidden layers** — Proposes a layered architecture moving from observed meme entities to extracted claims to inferred public narratives.
- **Ecological concepts transposed to memetic claims** — Uses life history, niche succession, and complex interactions from ecology to frame research questions about how memetic claims emerge, change and interact.
- **Fuzzy set formalism for multi-analyst claim annotation** — Proposes representing entity and claim annotations as fuzzy membership assertions so that disagreement between analysts becomes data rather than noise.

## Key Findings

Core contributions and results:

- The authors argue that image memes function as quasi-arguments whose truncated structure and visual boundary insulate them from counter-arguments.
- In their Toulmin analysis, the Bush/9/11 template meme claims the Ohio derailment was a government conspiracy, relying on an audience-supplied 9/11 'inside-job' warrant.
- DRE3 is presented as filling a gap by adding an intermediate rhetorical-semantic layer between data collection and inference of hidden public narratives.
- Under the fuzzy-set approach, conflicting classifications become measurements of polarization, volatility, or analyst differences rather than intercoder-reliability problems.
- The authors state that DRE3 elements can be incorporated piecemeal into existing meme knowledge graphs such as IMKG and MemeX.
