## Abstract

> This paper provides an analysis of Giorgio Agamben's book Homo Sacer in the tradition of Active Inference. Homo Sacer articulates the relationship between bare life and political existence in Western politics and metaphysics. Agamben argues that politics is founded on the inclusive exclusion of bare life, where natural biological life – the physiology and cognition of the body – is politicized...

## Methods

- **Close reading of Agamben's Homo Sacer** — Summarizes and contextualizes Agamben's account of bare life, sovereignty and the state of exception as the foundation for later sections.
- **Agamben-Kuhn analogy of exception and revolutionary science** — Treats Kuhnian paradigm shifts as analogous to the sovereign decision on the state of exception, both grounding what counts as valid.
- **Mapping via the Active Inference Ontology (affordances, VFE, EFE)** — Uses Active Inference Ontology terms to map the state of exception to affordances, bare life to VFE, and sovereign agency to EFE.
- **'Active Stateference' Python-style pseudocode** — Provides draft pseudocode of a single-agent generative model with lawful/unlawful/just/unjust states, a remapped model under exception, and a VFE loop.

## Key Findings

- Asserts that realized epistemic agency is grounded in the enacted policy selection of the cognitive sovereign.
- Argues that sovereigns in a state of exception gain exceptional affordances that emerge from new precision dynamics, sustained by perceiving the state as dangerous.
- Proposes that the subject's VFE-bare life connection grounds the sovereign's EFE-agency relationship through the state of exception.
- Concludes that, under 'Bayesian State Mechanics', the sovereign's expected free energy causally influences the subject's variational free energy.
- Notes that the current model considers only one agent and outlines extensions to multi-agent 'Active GovernAnts', cognitive security and quantum settings.
