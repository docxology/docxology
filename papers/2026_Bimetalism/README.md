## Methods

- **Overlay of British/American bimetallic history with Blake's prophetic corpus** — Reads monetary events from the 1717 Mint ratio to the 1896 'Cross of Gold' alongside Blake's illuminated works as parallel narratives.
- **Close reading of Blake's texts and plates against monetary acts** — Attends to Blake's specific textual attacks on gold, metallurgical imagery, and plate compositions while treating the 1797 and 1816 acts as 'topological fractures'.
- **Velde border conditions for bimetallic circulation** — Uses Velde's inequality on world monetary demand shares to frame the 1816 Coinage Act's tokenization of silver as violating the conditions for concurrent circulation.
- **Active Inference expected free energy as an analogy for value** — Maps the pragmatic/epistemic split of expected free energy onto gold/silver to propose an 'epistemic bimetallism', drawing also on Sabine's CRR framework.

## Key Findings

- The author reads Newton's 1717 Mint ratio (1:15.21) as an operationalization of 'Newton's sleep', a rigid prior that ignored evidence of silver's circulation.
- The essay argues the 1816 Coinage Act erased the bimetallic tension by tokenizing silver rather than synthesizing the two metals.
- It proposes epistemic bimetallism as a general norm for biological or institutional systems, avoiding both rigid 'Single Vision' and unconstrained scepticism.
- The conclusion frames epistemic bimetallism as a refusal to let any single abstraction stand in for the full valuation of persons, rather than a policy prescription.
