# Partner Pen Play in Parallel (PPPiP): A New Paradigm for Relationship Improvement

**Alexandra Mikhailova, Daniel A. Friedman** (2018) · Art & Synergetics

## Context

This work addresses topics in **Art & Synergetics**: PPPiP, art therapy, improvisation, intimate relationships.

## Methods

Primary methods and techniques applied in this work:

- **Definition and practice description of Partner Pen Play in Parallel** — Specifies PPPiP as two people simultaneously drawing on one sheet with pens, and outlines how sessions begin, proceed, and end.
- **Interdisciplinary synthesis from art therapy, sexology, neuroscience, aesthetics** — In the absence of direct studies, the authors synthesize prior research to argue a plausible scientific basis for PPPiP.
- **Mapping PPPiP onto Wadeson's five-element art therapy framework** — Re-enumerates Wadeson's five advantages of art therapy (Immediate, Genuine, Spatial Expression, Permanent, Shared Pleasure) and checks PPPiP against them.
- **Free Energy Principle framing of partner modeling (FEPPPPiP)** — Interprets joint drawing as building a generative model of the partner and as collective self-evidencing of the relationship.
- **Appendix of example drawings by the authors** — Presents author-made examples of the two PPPiP categories, fontplay and pure abstraction.

## Key Findings

Core contributions and results:

- The authors conclude PPPiP shares all five characteristics hypothesized to make art therapy effective in marital contexts.
- They propose a model in which PPPiP synergistically activates motor and cortico-limbic circuits, supported by imaging evidence from improvisational and aesthetic contexts.
- PPPiP is distinguished from traditional art therapy as less clinically oriented, with fewer external constraints and direct integration of art-making with relationship co-creation.
- The authors predict greater neural synchrony between partners than strangers, and in PPPiP versus isolated tandem drawing, as a testable future direction.
