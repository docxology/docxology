# The Great Preset: Remote Teams and Operational Art

**Daniel A. Friedman, Richard J. Cordes** (2020) · Cognitive Security

## Context

This work addresses topics in **Cognitive Security**: cognitive security, narrative ecosystems, Great Reset, sensemaking.

## Methods

Primary methods and techniques applied in this work:

- **Edited volume compiling COGSEC's 2020 work in six chapters** — Friedman and Cordes edit six chronologically ordered chapters by COGSEC and collaborators on remote teams, operations orders, and maps.
- **Complexity Science analysis of counterinsurgency team assembly (Ch. 1)** — Cordes and Friedman use a Complexity Science framework, starting from a 2008 Mumbai attack vignette, to study rapid team assembly in counterinsurgency.
- **Systems Engineering ONFT assessment with Active Inference (Ch. 3)** — Vyatkin et al. use Systems Engineering to define Ontologies, Narratives, Formal documents, and Tools (ONFT) for remote teams, explored via Active Inference.
- **Historical review of Operations Orders (Chs. 4 and 6)** — Chapters trace military and business Operations Orders from antiquity to the present to inform new catechism-style formats.
- **Responses to DARPA Polyplexus incubator prompts (Chs. 2 and 5)** — Chapters 2 and 5 were written in response to Polyplexus incubators on remote culture for pandemics and on reimagining maps.

## Key Findings

Core contributions and results:

- The editors report that Chapter 1 found rapid reorganization, resilience, and integration of new information channels to be instrumental for both terrorist and counterterrorist activity.
- Chapter 4 (Cordes and Friedman) found that changes in technological and organizational complexity historically required new OPORD formats, and proposes a Facilitator's Catechism building on the Heilmeier Catechism.
- Chapter 6 (Cordes, Friedman, Phelan) presents an Innovator's Catechism, a catechism-styled operations order for early-stage innovation teams derived from the Facilitator's Catechism.
- Chapter 2 contributes role-based improvisational and cultural dynamics for rapidly assembled online teams and initial "Infinite Game" structures for research and citizen science.
- The editors note COGSEC came to see knowledge management as fundamentally social and collaborative, beyond computational tools and data standards.
