## Methods

- **Archival analysis, secondary-source synthesis and quantitative data analysis** — Combines historical sources with census, labor, seismological and oceanographic data in one reproducible repository workflow.
- **Narrative history from published sources, without original fieldwork or interviews** — Relies on published secondary sources, archives, federal datasets and peer-reviewed science; Indigenous material is drawn from public published sources.
- **Four-lens nested-systems structure: Space, Time, People, Ideas** — Organizes forty-six topical chapters into four parts rather than one strict chronology, drawing on hierarchy, social-ecological and resilience theory.
- **Explicit evidence-class labeling in prose and figure captions** — Distinguishes counts, measured records, model outputs, post-event reconstructions and dated public-status claims.
- **Peng (2011) reproducibility workflow with registry-backed generated figures** — Versions analytical choices, twenty-four registry-backed figures, data and plotting scripts alongside the manuscript.

## Key Findings

- Argues that Crescent City is an emergent place in which Space, Time, People and Ideas continually alter one another.
- Frames the often-cited 37% fifty-year probability of an M>=8.0 southern Cascadia rupture as paleoseismic model output, not a deterministic forecast.
- Synthesizes that the 1964 Alaska tsunami killed eleven people in Crescent City, destroyed twenty-nine downtown blocks, and remains the deadliest on the contiguous U.S. Pacific coast.
- Describes the shift from a timber-and-fishing economy to one anchored by Pelican Bay State Prison, the hospital, schools, the parks and the Dungeness-crab fishery.
- Among four stated policy implications, argues that tribal co-management is an operational framework rather than a courtesy gesture.
