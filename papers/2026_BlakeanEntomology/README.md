## Abstract

> How do Blake's small lives make another being's needs matter? This article argues that provision, feeding and aggregation give different forms to that question: help can enable another life, appetite can use it, and a collective body can redistribute its powers. Copy-specific readings of four short poems, nine selected historical scans and bounded prophetic and prose passages test this account against symbolic, spiritual and human-centered alternatives. The emmet's offered route, the fly's reciprocal likeness and the Worm/infant comparison become visual arguments in which the complete impression remains beside the exact words, a rival reading and a limit on biological identification. A reproducible concordance supplies breadth without turning retrieval into a bestiary: 102 categories yield 36,039 category-source-span candidates in 2,067 captured units; 2,498 belong to 199 identified Blake-primary reading units. Within that scope, 82 name-family categories supply 1,239 candidates. Pooled and equal nonempty-group compositions differ by total variation 0.222387 across 26 positive groups. Source-role contrasts, finite proximity experiments and recorded reading windows expose dependence on particular inputs. The contextual ledger contains 47 authored decisions and leaves 35,992 candidates unreviewed. These measures describe a search and its selective examination, not animal prevalence or complete-oeuvre coverage. Baine's biological-symbolic scholarship, accessed here through a contemporary review, makes contextual variability a continuing challenge. The argument locates ethical force in particular acts of hearing, sustaining, injuring and joining; neither a shared name nor a modern lineage determines that force.
