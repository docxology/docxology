# Context-dependent expression of the foraging gene in field colonies of ants

**Krista K. Ingram, Deborah M. Gordon, Daniel A. Friedman, Michael Greene, John Kahler, Swetha Peteru** (2016) · Entomology

## Context

This work addresses topics in **Entomology**: foraging gene, Pogonomyrmex barbatus, harvester ants, gene expression.

## Methods

Primary methods and techniques applied in this work:

- **Field collection of Pogonomyrmex barbatus workers in five task/age groups** — Workers from mature colonies near Rodeo, New Mexico were sorted into callows, brood workers, nest maintenance workers, patrollers, and foragers.
- **24 h time-of-day sampling at 4 h intervals** — Ants of each task and colony were sampled every 4 h over a day and flash frozen to track daily changes in foraging gene expression.
- **Light exposure experiment: 13 L : 11 D versus constant darkness** — Foragers and brood workers from six field colonies were housed in artificial nest-boxes under ambient light/dark cycles or complete darkness for 24 h.
- **Brain qPCR of Pbfor and cycle, normalized to PbEF1a** — Whole-brain RNA was assayed by TaqMan qPCR using harvester-ant-specific primers, with elongation factor 1a as the control gene.
- **Mixed-model ANOVAs and sinusoidal contrast analysis of forager expression** — Expression over time was tested with mixed-model ANOVAs, and forager patterns were compared to a sinusoid approximating daily foraging activity.

## Key Findings

Core contributions and results:

- Foraging mRNA levels are linked to changes over weeks in worker age, location, and task, and show significant daily oscillations in foragers.
- Foragers have high foraging mRNA levels during daylight hours, when they are most active outside the nest.
- Under a 13 L : 11 D light cycle, oscillations appeared in foragers but not in brood workers.
- In constant darkness (DD), foraging expression showed no significant change over time in either task.
- In both field and experimentally treated foragers, foraging expression was significantly correlated with expression of the circadian clock gene cycle.
