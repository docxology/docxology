# Emergent Teams for Complex Threats

**Richard J. Cordes, Daniel Ari Friedman** (2020) · Cognitive Security

## Context

This work addresses topics in **Cognitive Security**: emergent teams, operations orders, team formation, innovation.

## Methods

Primary methods and techniques applied in this work:

- **Complexity Science framing of counterinsurgency and team assembly** — Applies a Complexity Science lens to link rapid team assembly with counterinsurgency and counterterrorism outcomes.
- **Case vignette of the 2008 Lashkar-e-Taiba attack on Mumbai** — Reconstructs the Mumbai attack from secondary sources to contrast attackers' OSINT-driven improvisation with responders' inflexibility.
- **Synthesis of High Reliability Organization (HRO) literature** — Draws on HRO and emergency-response work (e.g. tiger teams, Toyota SMART teams) on organizational fluidity and reorganization.
- **Historical comparisons: Malayan Emergency and 1993 New York plot** — Uses the Malayan Emergency reorganization and the foiled 1993 New York landmarks plot as contrasting examples of rapid reorganization.

## Key Findings

Core contributions and results:

- Across the analyses reviewed, the authors identify superior information and OSINT exploitation for rapid planning and reorganization as the common explanation for the Mumbai attackers' success.
- The authors note the Indian government had the same information as the attackers but did not assemble specialists who could have used it.
- They introduce 'Complex Threat Surfaces' in place of 'attack surfaces' to stress integrated management of varied non-linear failure modes, including non-adversarial events.
- They argue that destroying central leadership of decentralized groups may cause fracture and increased complexity rather than collapse.
- The paper concludes that the ability to rapidly reorganize, propagate information, and reassemble teams differentiated event outcomes, and calls for further work on information-sharing design and OSINT.
