# PhD: Behavioral, Physiological, and Transcriptomic Variation Among Colonies of Pogonomyrmex barbatus

**Daniel Ari Friedman** (2019) · Entomology

## Context

This work addresses topics in **Entomology**: PhD dissertation, Pogonomyrmex barbatus, collective behavior, transcriptomics.

## Methods

Primary methods and techniques applied in this work:

- **RNA-seq of pooled forager brains from 6 P. barbatus colonies (Chapter 1)** — Three libraries per colony, each pooling three forager brains, compared colonies that did or did not strongly reduce foraging on dry days.
- **kallisto/sleuth expression analysis with Blast2GO annotation and Cytoscape coexpression** — Reads were pseudoaligned to the P. barbatus reference transcriptome with kallisto and analyzed in sleuth; GO enrichment used Blast2GO and a coexpression network was built in Cytoscape.
- **Oral dopamine administration with mass-spectrometry brain quantification** — Single ants were fed water or dopamine solutions and brain dopamine titers were quantified by mass spectrometry with a labeled internal standard.
- **Field counts of foraging trips by paint-marked dopamine- vs control-treated foragers** — Marked foragers given 0 or 3 mg/mL dopamine in PBS were returned to their nests and their foraging trips counted the next morning in 30-second intervals.
- **Hydration manipulations, single-brain RNA-seq and individual-trip analyses (Chapters 2-4)** — Later chapters tested forager desiccation physiology and exogenous hydration, RNA-seq on single forager brains, and trip distributions of individually marked treated ants.

## Key Findings

Core contributions and results:

- Forager brains from different colonies varied significantly in biogenic amine titers and in expression of neurophysiological signaling pathways involved in foraging regulation.
- Raising forager brain dopamine increased foraging activity, while lowering brain dopamine decreased it.
- Foragers from colonies that reduce foraging in dry conditions lose water and motor coordination faster; hydrated foragers made more trips, especially as conditions got drier.
- Hundreds of genes had expression patterns correlated with colony traits; loci more central to coexpression networks tended to correlate better with colony traits and showed greater coding-sequence constraint.
- The stimulatory effect of hydration and dopamine on foraging trips came from slight increases across many treated ants, not from a small subset.
